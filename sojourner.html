THE MARS ARCHIVE · 1997

Sojourner

The little rover that made history as the first successful wheeled robotic rover to operate on Mars.

Mars Pathfinder mission · NASA

01 — The Beginning

On July 4, 1997, NASA's Mars Pathfinder spacecraft landed on the surface of Mars. It carried a small robotic rover named Sojourner.

Sojourner was designed to explore the surrounding terrain, examine Martian rocks and soil, and send scientific information back to Earth.

04 — What Did It Discover?

Sojourner examined Martian rocks and soil using its scientific instruments. Its observations helped scientists study the composition of rocks and understand the geology of the landing area.

The mission also demonstrated that a small mobile robot could successfully explore another planet and communicate its findings to Earth.

05 — Why Was It Left Behind?

Sojourner was not designed to return to Earth. It operated as part of the Mars Pathfinder mission, using the lander as its communication link to Earth.

After communication with the Pathfinder lander was lost in September 1997, the mission ended. Sojourner remained on Mars, becoming one of the planet's historic human-made artifacts.

06 — Its Legacy

Sojourner helped prove that robotic rovers could explore the Martian surface. Its success helped pave the way for later missions, including Spirit, Opportunity, Curiosity, and Perseverance.

Although its mission ended decades ago, its contribution to Mars exploration continues to matter.

07 — Still on Mars

Sojourner remains on Mars near the Pathfinder landing site. It is no longer operating, but it is still part of the history of planetary exploration.
