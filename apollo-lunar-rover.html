THE MOON ARCHIVE · 1971–1972

Apollo Lunar Rover

The battery-powered vehicle that let Apollo astronauts travel farther across the Moon and bring more of its story home.

Searching NASA's Image and Video Library…

01 — The Beginning

The Lunar Roving Vehicle (LRV), often called the Moon buggy, was a lightweight electric vehicle carried to the Moon on Apollo 15, Apollo 16 and Apollo 17. Folded for transport in the Lunar Module, it was unfolded by astronauts after landing. It carried astronauts, tools and lunar samples across terrain that would have been difficult to explore on foot.

04 — What Did It Help Us Discover?

The rover extended the distance astronauts could travel from their landing sites. Crews could visit more geological features, collect a wider range of lunar samples and carry scientific equipment. NASA's records report that the Apollo 17 rover covered 35.9 kilometres in total.

05 — Why Was It Left Behind?

The LRV was designed for surface exploration, not a return journey to Earth. The astronauts used the Lunar Module to leave the Moon, while the rover stayed at the landing site. Bringing a bulky vehicle home was not part of the mission design.

06 — Where Is It Now?

Three LRVs remain on the Moon at the Apollo 15, Apollo 16 and Apollo 17 landing sites. They are inactive artifacts, not functioning vehicles. A fourth rover was built as a spare and did not fly to the Moon.

07 — Its Legacy

The LRV showed that crewed lunar exploration could include mobile field science. It remains an example of engineering for vacuum, low gravity, extreme temperatures and dusty terrain.
