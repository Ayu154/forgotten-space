THE MARS ARCHIVE · 2003–2011

Spirit

A robotic geologist that studied rocks, climbed hills and found evidence that ancient Mars had environments shaped by water.

01 — The Beginning

Spirit was one of NASA's twin Mars Exploration Rovers. It launched in 2003 and landed in Gusev Crater in January 2004. Its planned mission was about 90 Martian days, or sols. Spirit was sent to study rocks and soil for clues about Mars' watery past and to test technologies for mobile robotic exploration.

04 — What Did It Help Us Discover?

Spirit found strong evidence that parts of ancient Mars had been altered by water. It examined rocks, soil and minerals with cameras and instruments, including a tool that ground into rock surfaces so scientists could study what was underneath. Its findings revealed a more complex geological history than the dry landscape visible today might suggest.

05 — Why Was It Left Behind?

Spirit was designed to operate on Mars, not to return to Earth. In 2009 it became stuck in soft soil and could no longer drive to a position that would help it survive the Martian winter. After its last communication in March 2010, NASA continued trying to contact it before ending recovery efforts in May 2011.

06 — Where Is It Now?

Spirit remains on Mars at a location in the western part of Gusev Crater, where it became stuck. It is inactive. NASA officially concluded efforts to contact the rover on May 25, 2011.

07 — Its Legacy

Spirit operated for more than six years, far beyond its planned 90-sol mission. Together with its twin Opportunity, it transformed understanding of ancient Martian environments and demonstrated how long-lived mobile robots could conduct field geology on another planet.
